Follow an idea from source to decision.

Galore brings public trades, beliefs, and past performance together. Start with a post or person, see the sources and possible investments, then choose what to follow in a paper account.

What can I do on Galore today?

Explore creators like Jeremy Lefebvre and Jim Cramer with their full records, losing calls included, and follow disclosed holdings from people like Nancy Pelosi, Warren Buffett and Cathie Wood. Paste a post to see the belief behind it and the companies that express it. Test Your Belief shows how an idea would have performed on past prices.

You can practise with a paper portfolio in the Galore app. Live brokerage trading is not available yet. Galore does not hold your money or place real-money trades today.

How is Galore different?

Other apps earn when you trade more and show the creator's return. Galore shows yours, after fees, losing calls included. You set the amount and the limits, and nothing is bought without your approval. You can pause any time.

What does it cost?

One membership is required to trade, with the same features on both plans: $89.99 a year, about $7.50 a month, or $11.99 a month. A flat fee, so there is no push to trade. See pricing.

Who is building Galore?

Galore is built by Mehek Mandal. Our mission is to make investment knowledge accessible so people can build wealth their way.

What happens when I join the waitlist?

We save your name and email for Galore launch updates. Confirm your email to open a $10,000 paper account. Live brokerage trading is reserved for a separate approved pilot and is not available to everyone.

After a new signup, your confirmation includes a private removal link. Read the waitlist privacy notice for details.

Have a question?

Send Galore a message. The form reaches our monitored inbox, and we can reply to the email address you provide.

What should I know before investing?

Galore is not registered with the SEC or FINRA and does not give investment advice. Returns shown for people you can follow are model returns from public filings and posts, not their actual accounts. AI can make mistakes, ideas from creators can be wrong, and investing involves risk, including loss of principal. Past performance is not a promise of future returns.
